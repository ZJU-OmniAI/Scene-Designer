SCENE-DESIGNERResearch overview · English narration

01 / OVERVIEW

Design intelligence.
Scene design and scene critique.

We define design intelligence as the ability to both design a scene and critique its quality.

Scene design concerns constructing physically valid, functionally correct, and layout-coherent scenes, while efficiently correcting errors without unnecessary trial and error when problems arise.

Scene critique focuses on assessing scene quality along the same dimensions and distinguishing better designs from poorer ones.

These two capabilities are closely connected, since effective design requires reliable critique to guide construction, while meaningful critique requires an understanding of what constitutes good scene design.

02 / MOTIVATION

Where general-purpose models
fall short.

Two diagnostic studies reveal limitations in both scene design and scene critique.

Challenge 1: Inefficient correction caused by unnecessary trial and error.

Across 100 scenes generated by GPT-5.2, even straightforward construction errors often trigger multiple corrective attempts. Recognizing an error is not enough: effective scene design requires the experience to correct it efficiently.

Challenge 2: Insufficient scene critique ability.

Reliable scene critique must assess physical validity, functional correctness, and layout coherence. General-purpose models can overlook functional relationships and coherent organization when comparing scenes.

03 / THE METHOD

Learn to design.
Learn to critique.

Two kinds of experience build a shared foundation. Scene-GRPO then uses two complementary local rewards to select better design choices and reduce unnecessary trial and error.

Improvement reward

Compares each child scene with its parent to determine whether the edit actually improves scene quality, discouraging ineffective modifications.

Causal Trajectory Consolidation +

Error-aware candidate identification flags potentially ineffective operations. Correction-effect evaluation preserves effective decisions and necessary intermediate states, removing only ineffective operations without downstream contributions. Replaying the trajectory verifies the same final scene.

Learning scene preferences +

Scene-OTC uses mild layout deviations, functional degradation, and severe physical violations. Accumulated perturbations and different strengths on the same object allow automatic ranking. Applying the same perturbation to different objects requires manual annotation.

Two complementary local rewards +

The branch-ranking reward compares sibling scenes; the improvement reward compares each child scene with its parent. Format, global length, and object-completeness rewards support efficient, complete scene construction. The Critic remains frozen during Scene-GRPO.

DATASET CONSTRUCTION

Learning from design and critique experience.

Scene-OTA-35K captures effective construction decisions, while Scene-OTC-15K provides scene preference comparisons. Together, they teach the model how to design a scene and how to assess its quality.

Scene-OTA construction. Causal Trajectory Consolidation removes ineffective operations without downstream contributions while retaining effective decisions and necessary intermediate states. Replaying the consolidated trajectory verifies that it reaches the same final scene.

Scene-OTC construction. Scene perturbations create preference comparisons through mild layout deviations, functional degradation, and severe physical violations, providing supervision across complementary dimensions of scene quality.

TRAINING STRATEGY

From Scene-Builder to Scene-Designer.

Joint supervised fine-tuning establishes a shared foundation for scene design and critique. Scene-GRPO then expands multiple candidate edits at each step and uses a frozen Critic to guide the policy toward better design decisions.

Choose among alternatives. The branch-ranking reward compares sibling scenes generated from the same parent, encouraging the policy to select higher-quality actions.

Verify actual progress. The improvement reward compares each child scene with its parent, discouraging ineffective modifications. Global length and object-completeness rewards encourage efficient, complete scene construction.

SCENE DESIGN · QUALITATIVE EVALUATION

How well does the model design?

Compare generated scenes under the same instructions across seven methods. These examples illustrate differences in physical validity, functional correctness, and layout coherence, from existing approaches to Scene-Builder, Scene-Designer, and Scene-Designer with test-time Critic guidance.

SCENE CRITIQUE · QUANTITATIVE EVALUATION

How well does the model critique?

On the Scene-OTC test sets, evaluators identify the better scene in pairwise comparisons and predict the complete preference order of four scenes. Joint training on design and critique experience achieves the highest accuracy in both tasks.
